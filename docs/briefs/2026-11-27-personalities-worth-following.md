# Brief (owner, 2026-11-27, queued "for later / soon"): the science and art of personalities worth following

**Status:** queued, not started. It was filed while the camera-conditioning and direction-to-images steps were pending. It authorises no new providers, spend, publishing or launches; existing research and production controls apply.

**The universal principle:** every personality offers a compelling reason to follow them. How it does so stays an open creative question.

The brief should shape Casting, Talent Management, Creative Direction, Research, Production and the Brain's experiments, without new taxonomies, rigid scoring or universal mandates.

## What the owner asked

1. **Why people follow personalities.**
   - The psychology, sociology, behavioural science and culture behind following someone.
   - Motivations include attraction, aspiration, emulation, fascination, entertainment, curiosity, resonance, relatability, inspiration, discovery, expertise, familiarity, cultural relevance, connection, an unfolding story and a distinctive worldview.
   - **Aspiration, broadly:** confidence, charisma, freedom, style, taste, intelligence, humour, fitness, accomplishment, a way of experiencing the world, not only wealth or beauty. Do not assume it dominates; find when entertainment, relatability, utility or curiosity are stronger.
2. **The audience journey:** attention → interest → conversion (the follow) → retention → affinity → commercial influence. These are distinct outcomes. Every personality answers *"what does someone expect to experience in the future that makes following worthwhile today?"* Individual pieces of content can serve different purposes.
3. **Attractiveness is necessary for many, but insufficient.**
   - Distinctive, recognisable and memorable: from a photo, a few seconds of video, a line or a mannerism.
   - Through appearance, grooming, styling, signatures, palette, expressions, posture, voice, speech, humour, rituals, interests, worldview, environments and presentation.
   - No forced gimmicks. Mainstream editorial standards, never unnecessarily sexualised.
4. **The distinctive qualities of AI, selectively.**
   - A spectrum: photoreal → heightened → stylised but human → cinematic or editorial → fantastical → fictional characters.
   - No mandate either way.
   - Deliberate stylisation must be told apart from artifacts, identity drift, unnatural movement and poor execution.
5. **Interesting lives, not content calendars.** What makes each personality fascinating: what they care about and pursue, their perspective, the experiences audiences would enjoy, unexpected depth, what audiences would anticipate next, how they evolve. The full spectrum, from a coherent lifestyle to evolving stories.
6. **Synthetic existence as a creative advantage**, not only a cost advantage: extraordinary lives, unusual occupations, remarkable environments, alternative eras and worlds, ambitious projects, adventures, synthetic identity as part of the persona. *What would audiences love to encounter repeatedly that we can create unusually well because we are AI-native?*
7. **Real-world relevance and fictional worlds are compatible.**
   - Culturally current with fictional lives.
   - Coherent histories, recurring places and projects, narrative progression where it fits.
   - Relationships between our personalities, occasional portfolio interactions, optional shared worlds.
   - Never forced. Always openly virtual. Fiction never presented as verified real events, relationships, endorsements or experiences.
8. **Depth, contrast and unexpected combinations.** For example, an elegant gentleman with mischievous humour, or a glamorous personality fascinated by engineering. Find when contrast increases appeal and when it undermines coherence; no gimmick collections. This ties to the work on character embodiment and behavioural temperature.
9. **Sustainable content engines:** evolving lifestyle, recurring experiences, expertise, perspective on culture, projects, recurring humour, adventures, taste and recommendations, experimentation, relationships, narrative, visual spectacle. More complexity is not better; a photo, a caption or a carousel can carry a fascinating life.
10. **Case studies through approved web research:**
    - virtual personalities: Lil Miquela, Aitana López, Imma, Granny Spills, Chloe VS History, Nobody Sausage, Lu do Magalu, and others;
    - relevant human creators, fictional celebrities and franchises;
    - their propositions, first attention, why people followed, differentiation, recognisable identity, how they sustained interest, cultural participation, storytelling and relationships, distribution and collaborations, commercial effects, struggles and decline.

    Separate durable principles from novelty, first-mover advantage, existing distribution and paid promotion. Avoid survivorship bias by including the failures. No scraping and no third-party media annotation.
11. **The science:** media and consumer psychology, social comparison, aspirational consumption, parasocial relationships, celebrity psychology, character attachment and identification, social identity, narrative transportation, curiosity, novelty vs familiarity, humour, aesthetic perception, attractiveness, charisma, memorability, distinctiveness, authenticity, trust, loyalty, brand affinity, virtual-influencer and HCI research. Include contradictions and limitations. Never formulas or manipulative tactics.
12. **Casting and Talent Management.** Make the existing mechanisms more intelligent rather than adding taxonomies. Casting reasons about:
    - attention and why follow;
    - memorability; attractive, fascinating or aspirational qualities;
    - emotional and experiential value; distinctiveness; an interesting life or perspective;
    - creative freedom; sustainability; how the relationship evolves; commerce;
    - what we do unusually well as a synthetic studio;
    - feasibility and economics.

    This is judgement, not a checklist.
13. **Hypotheses to test, not facts:**
    - distinctive visuals improve memorability and conversion;
    - attractiveness plus unusual personality;
    - aspiration drives attention and personality drives retention;
    - stylised can beat photoreal in some categories;
    - recurring experiences give reasons to return;
    - cultural relevance aids discovery;
    - world-building increases investment for some propositions;
    - extraordinary synthetic experiences;
    - a coherent lifestyle without story;
    - unexpected traits add entertainment without losing aspiration;
    - different mixes of aspiration, attraction, humour and utility produce different relationships.

    Before launch: execution, distinctiveness, perceived quality, feasibility and internal evaluations. After launch: first-party outcomes. AI judgements of appeal are not audience preference.
14. **A diverse portfolio:** realistic, stylised, aspirational, humorous, entertaining, useful, culturally influential, resonant, mysterious, narrative, fantastical, or simple and superbly executed. Not every creator a fictional celebrity with a backstory.
15. **Connect it to the modular model architecture.** Each capability (research, casting, writing, embodiment, direction, visual, spatial and cinematographic, production, evaluation, the Brain) improves independently, using the best viable model per task.

## Deliverables requested

1. A research-backed assessment of why people follow and invest in personalities.
2. Case studies, successful and unsuccessful, with transferable lessons.
3. Aspiration, attractiveness, distinctiveness, memorability, entertainment and character depth.
4. The creative advantages of synthetic existence.
5. Realism vs deliberate stylisation and distinctive AI aesthetics.
6. Recommendations for Casting, Talent Management and Creative Direction.
7. Whether the architecture supports evolving fictional lives, recurring experiences, cultural relevance and optional shared worlds.
8. Testable creative hypotheses for first-party experimentation.
9. How the Brain connects this research to decisions and future audience outcomes.
10. Practical improvements to agent instructions, capabilities and workflows within current authorisation.
