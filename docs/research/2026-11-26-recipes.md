## What it is for

Distill (`intelligence.cluster`) finds ingredients: single coded attributes that high-traction posts carry more often
than low ones. The owner wants recipes. A recipe is a combination of attributes that works together, for a kind of
creator and a medium, in a context. The engine should report how sure it is. It must not invent findings by testing
thousands of combinations. The Brain should be the one asking the questions.

## The method in plain words

**Outcome.** Each post's engagement over its own creator's median (`over_own_median`), on a log scale. A creator who
is simply bigger, or whose audience likes everything, is divided out before anything is compared. A ratio of 1.25
means a post did 25% better than that creator usually does.

- A post without the number is left out and counted, with the reason (`no_public_counts`: the platform showed no
  counts; `no_creator_baseline`: counts exist but the creator's median was never computed). It is never read as
  zero.
- A near-zero ratio is raised to `min_ratio` before the log, so one dead post cannot dominate, and is counted
  (`clipped`).

**Which fields a post can speak to.**

- A field the medium is not asked is not there for that post. Video fields on a photo are left out, not counted as
  "absent".
- An `unknown` value leaves the field out for that post.
- An empty label list counts as a read that saw none of the labels.
- Each read stores a short hash of every field's value list (`observed.vocab_versions`). A field is used only from
  reads whose hash is the current one. If a list is renamed or shortened, "not a blazer" no longer means the same
  thing, so older reads drop out for that field and are counted (`old_vocab`). v1 reads, and v2 reads made before
  versioning, have no hashes (`unversioned`). When values were only added, the owner can list the old hash in
  `intelligence.annotation.vocab_compatible` and those reads count again.

**Four questions the analysis can answer.**

| Function | Question |
|---|---|
| `main_effect(rows, attr)` | Do posts with it do better than posts without it, within the same creator? |
| `interaction(rows, a, b)` | Does the pair add something beyond each one alone? (both − a only − b only + neither) |
| `recipe(rows, [a, b, c])` | Do posts with the whole combination (at most 3) do better than the rest? |
| `conditional(rows, attr, context)` | Does the effect differ by context (medium, platform, archetype, follower band, month, post age band, or any coded single-value field such as environment)? |

`conditional` gives the effect within each context value. It also runs a heterogeneity test: a Wald test that the
context effects are equal, with the covariance taken from a joint bootstrap over creators, since contexts can share
creators.

## Why the creator is the unit

Posts from one creator are not independent. They share her face, audience, style, posting habits and luck. A test
that counts posts lets a creator with 300 posts outvote ten creators with 30 each. It also lets one creator's quirk
look like a law. The test `test_a_single_creator_artefact_is_not_a_finding` builds that case: one creator's sunglasses
posts do 7x and nobody else's differ. A post-level comparison is significant (z > 3). The creator-level test gives
p = 1, because it is one creator out of sixteen.

How the creator-level estimate is built:

1. Within each creator, and within each medium (and any `covariates` from policy), the analysis compares mean
   outcomes. It needs at least `min_posts_per_arm` posts in every arm (every cell of an interaction).
2. A creator's strata are combined, weighted by how precise each contrast is.
3. Each creator then counts once, and the creators' effects are averaged with equal weight.

Uncertainty is measured over creators:

- **CI:** a bootstrap that resamples creators (seeded, `bootstrap.iterations`).
- **p:** a sign-flip permutation test of the creators' effects. It is exact for up to `exact_flip_max_creators`
  creators, and uses random flips above that.
- **Sign test:** a plain count of creators up vs down, reported beside p.

**Floors.** A test runs only with at least `min_creators` creators with eligible posts and `min_creators_both_arms`
creators that fill every arm. Below that it is `insufficient`, never a weak "maybe".

**Confounders we stratify or show.**

- Stratifying within creator x medium removes creator-level and format-level differences.
- For each arm, the result shows the share of posts by posting month and by post age band (how old the post was
  when its counts were read). If one arm is all from one month, or all fresh posts, you can see it.
- `covariates` (month, age_band, platform) can be added as extra strata.

## Discovery and confirmation

Creators are split once, by a hash of their id (`split_salt`, `holdout_share`), into **discovery** and **holdout**.
The split never changes after results are read.

1. **Declare the family.** The run's tests are fixed before any result is seen. These are a question's
   pre-registered spec, plus the screen's tests when it asks for one. The family size is that count, including
   tests that turn out to lack data (they count as p = 1).
2. **Discovery.** Every test runs on the discovery creators. Benjamini-Hochberg over the whole family at `fdr_q`.
3. **Holdout.** Only tests that pass are retested on the holdout creators. BH again over those, and the effect must
   point the same way.
4. **Stages.**

| Stage | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `confirmed` | Passed discovery and held on holdout. |
| `discovery` | Passed discovery, but the holdout has too little data. UNCONFIRMED, and labelled so in every brief. |
| `failed` | Not significant in discovery, or not replicated on holdout. |
| `insufficient` | Too little data to test. |

**The screen** (`screen`) is the bounded data-driven part.

- It takes attributes held by between `prevalence_floor` and 1 − `prevalence_floor` of the discovery posts that know
  the field. This is chosen without looking at outcomes.
- It keeps the most balanced `max_attributes` of them.
- It tests their main effects and the pairwise interactions between attributes of different fields, at most
  `max_pairs`.
- Every one of those tests is in the family.

Noise has to get through two gates: FDR over the whole family, then replication on creators it has never seen.
`test_a_pure_noise_screen_of_many_pairs_confirms_nothing` runs 42 tests on pure noise and confirms none.

## What a result says

Each result carries:

- **effect**, as a multiplier on engagement over the creator's own median (1.30 = 30% above her usual), and its
  **CI** (95% by default).
- **n_posts**, **n_creators**, **n_creators_both_arms** (creators that actually contribute), and creators up and
  down.
- **sign_p**, **p** (permutation) and **q** (BH, from the stage it rests on).
- **stage** and **reason**.
- **discovery** and **holdout** blocks with their own numbers.
- **coverage**:
  - the share of posts with the metric, and why the others are missing;
  - per field, how many reads could not be used (unversioned, old_vocab, unknown);
  - the vocabulary hashes used.
- **balance** (each arm's months and age bands).

The top-level estimate is the holdout's once a test was retested. The discovery estimate of a selected test is
inflated (winner's curse); the holdout's is not.

`research_questions.brief(conn, policy, use)` turns answered questions into plain lines for agents, confirmed first.
A typical line: "[confirmed on holdout creators] garments=blazer together with lighting_kind=golden_hour, beyond each
alone: engagement x1.8 over each creator's own median (95% CI 1.6-2.0), 8 creators (8 up, 0 down), 384 posts,
q=0.008; 100% of the posts asked had engagement metrics".

## Questions the Brain asks

There is no new agent. The workflow:

- **Filing.** Any agent calls `research_questions.ask(conn, policy, requester, question, spec=None, kind=...)`. The
  kind is `data`, `web` or `both`.
  - A spec, when given, is checked against the coded catalogue. It is then stored before anything is tested
    (pre-registered), along with what was dropped.
  - A data question with no spec gets one cheap structured call (`llm.roles.research_question`, Haiku). The call
    turns the question into tests over the catalogue. Unknown attribute, context, medium and archetype names are
    dropped and listed. The spec is stored before the tests run.
- **Answering.** The intelligence agent's `questions` mode (`run_due`) answers what is queued:
  - data questions by the recipe analysis, on posts read through `evidence.annotated_posts`;
  - web questions by the research desk.
- **Budget.** Every call is charged to agent `research:questions` within `intelligence.questions.daily_usd`. A
  question whose call the day cannot afford stays queued.
- **Proposing.** `propose(conn, policy, llm, requester, context)` asks the model for at most `max_per_propose`
  testable questions, given the catalogue and what is already answered. Each is filed with its spec.

Consumers are not wired yet. The talent manager, showrunner, style director and casting would read `brief`, and the
CEO or talent manager would call `propose` or `ask`.

## Known limits

- **Observational.** These are other people's public posts. A confirmed recipe is a correlation that held on a
  second set of creators. It is not a cause.
- **Confounders that remain:**
  - Trends. A look and a sound may both have been rising the same fortnight. Month strata help only partly.
  - Collaborations and features: another creator's audience lifts a post.
  - Paid boosts. Promoted posts carry spend we cannot see.
  - Platform distribution changes.
  - Selection. Scouting picks creators who already work, and the monitor reads only posts its filters select.
  - The read itself. Annotation errors are larger on weak fields (nails, candid level, subject scale: see the
    vision benchmark), and noisy codes bias effects toward 1.
- **Engagement over own median is not reach, follows or sales.** Saves, shares and views are often missing on
  public data.
- **Power.** With few creators per side, a real effect can be `insufficient` or `failed`. The floors and the split
  trade power for honesty on purpose.
- **Heterogeneity across many contexts** is one test per question. The per-context effects are descriptive, not
  separately corrected.

## From a confirmed recipe to `validated`

Only a `confirmed` result can back a `correlation` card. `recipes.card_tests(result)` gives a card's evidence test,
with the holdout q and creators as the unit, and nothing for any other stage. Distill does not write recipe cards
yet: wiring that is the next step.

`validated` stays reserved for our own posts' real outcomes (`patterns.basis`). The path:

1. A confirmed recipe becomes a hypothesis for our own creators.
2. The talent manager or showrunner schedules posts with the recipe and comparable posts without it, the same
   creator in the same weeks.
3. The prediction is filed in `brain/predictions.py` (effect and CI from the recipe).
4. The posts' real outcomes, read through `evidence.py`, are scored against it.
5. When enough of our own posts back it, the card's `outcomes` make it `validated`. If they don't, the recipe is
   recorded as not transferring to AI creators, which is a finding too.
