# Fix the slow stages before chasing quality

As of November 2026, the engine's biggest cost-per-accepted-output wins come from fixing the two stages that barely produce anything, not from upgrading the cheap stage that already works. **Talking video** is the worst. InfiniteTalk at default settings runs 120 network evaluations per window. Its own documented 4-step lightx2v LoRA should cut that 15–30x, and two newer permissively licensed successors exist: SoulX-FlashTalk (Apache-2.0) and LongCat-Video-Avatar 1.6 (MIT). **Story clips** come next. Fourteen minutes per 5 s clip on an undistilled Wan 2.2 TI2V-5B is far off the pace now that 4-step distillation is standard, and hosted endpoints on stronger models sell a 5 s 720p clip for $0.10–$0.40. **Images** are already the cheapest credible option at roughly $0.0002–0.0004 per image (Z-Image Turbo, Apache-2.0). They are about 100 Elo behind FLUX 3 Image Klein, which is free for commercial use only under $5M revenue. **Voice** should move from shared Kokoro presets to designed-then-cloned voices on Apache-2.0 models (Qwen3-TTS, VoxCPM2), with each full script voiced in one pass. **The Brain** can save roughly half its Claude bill with no change in quality by using prompt caching and Batch, before any open model takes a role. Across every modality the binding constraint is licence text, not price: several of the best open-weight models are non-commercial or territory-restricted, and the owner has to accept a few revenue-capped community licences before the best open options can be used. Elo leaderboards do not measure identity retention, so every adoption below has to be decided by the engine's own gates on cost per accepted output.

## Talking video is blocked by settings, not by the model choice

No talking clip has finished, and the arithmetic explains why. InfiniteTalk's defaults are about 40 steps with text CFG 5 and audio CFG 4, which is three passes per step. The README documents a lightx2v LoRA that needs only **4 steps at text CFG 1 / audio CFG 2**. It also warns that the 8-step FusionX LoRA "worsens color shift" and weakens identity, so that one should be avoided ([InfiniteTalk GitHub](https://github.com/MeiGen-AI/InfiniteTalk)). Community figures are unverified but point the same way. One RTX 4090 user reports **40 minutes for a 10 s 480p clip** without a LoRA ([issue #210](https://github.com/MeiGen-AI/InfiniteTalk/issues/210)). A ComfyUI tutorial reports **about 70 s on a 4090** with the 4-step LoRA ([Next Diffusion](https://www.nextdiffusion.ai/tutorials/create-lip-sync-videos-from-images-with-infinitetalk-in-comfyui)). The ~94 GB download per boot is a second, separate cost. InfiniteTalk documents fp8 single-GPU quantization, so one fp8 14B checkpoint plus LoRAs should shrink that download substantially. That is an inference to verify in the smoke test. InfiniteTalk's licence is Apache-2.0, and its image-to-video mode is "good for up to about one minute" before colour shift grows. The project's segment design already works around that limit.

The field has also moved on. A 2026 comparison in the AptAvatar paper puts InfiniteTalk at **120 NFE** (network evaluations) against **4 NFE for SoulX-FlashTalk**. FlashTalk scores the best short-clip lip sync of the released models (Sync-C 7.74 vs InfiniteTalk's 7.17), while InfiniteTalk keeps the edge on minute-long clips (Sync-C 8.18 vs 7.98). Wan2.2-S2V is clearly worse on sync (5.63) ([AptAvatar](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24013v2)). AptAvatar itself leads the table at 2 NFE, but the paper does not say whether weights will be released. SoulX-FlashTalk-14B is Apache-2.0 per its model card ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Soul-AILab/SoulX-FlashTalk-14B/raw/main/README.md)). **LongCat-Video-Avatar 1.6**, released 2026-11-15, is MIT. It adds DMD/OPSD distill LoRAs, reduces error build-up over long takes, and claims more than 10 minutes through continuation segments, though it publishes no VRAM or speed figures ([LongCat-Video GitHub](https://github.com/meituan-longcat/LongCat-Video)). Wan2.2-S2V needs at least 80 GB VRAM even with offload, and HunyuanVideo-Avatar inherits a Tencent licence that is reported (unverified) to exclude the EU, UK and South Korea. Both should be deprioritised.

Hosted talking-head APIs are now cheap enough to serve as a fallback and as the gate's quality ceiling. A vendor-run survey (Stork sells a competing API) lists **WaveSpeed InfiniteTalk-Fast at $0.015/s**, Replicate wan-2.2-s2v at $0.02/s, Hedra Character-3 at $0.025–0.0625/s, Kling AI Avatar at about $0.056/s and OmniHuman 1.5 at $0.12–0.16/s ([Stork survey](https://www.stork.ai/blog/talking-head-api-pricing-2026)). fal's own InfiniteTalk endpoint is marked deprecated ([fal](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/infinitalk)), and the Sora 2 API shut down on 2026-09-24 ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/sora-2)). A 30 s clip costs about $0.45 raw on WaveSpeed. A distilled self-hosted model at 10–20 GPU-seconds per output second would cost roughly $0.06–0.12 for the same clip. That figure is an estimate built on illustrative hourly rates, so the hosted price is the bar self-hosting has to beat once boot and download time are counted. The commercial terms of these providers for synthetic people were not verified, and that has to happen before any of them is adopted.

A two-stage route is the fully commercial alternative: generate a silent clip, then dub the mouth. **MuseTalk 1.5** has MIT code, weights "available for any purpose, even commercially," a 256 px face region and a 4 GB VRAM footprint ([MuseTalk](https://github.com/TMElyralab/MuseTalk)). LatentSync 1.6 works at 512 px and needs 18 GB ([LatentSync](https://github.com/bytedance/LatentSync)). The weakness is that the body does not move with the speech. A stronger hybrid feeds a silent story clip into InfiniteTalk's video-to-video mode, which is documented as unlimited in length.

### Automated lip-sync scoring has a licensable path

No permissive drop-in for classic SyncNet exists. The VGG SyncNet weights are research-only under CC-BY, and the MIT code licence "does not cover the weights" ([syncnet-python](https://pypi.org/project/syncnet-python/)). The practical scorer is LatentSync's **`stable_syncnet.pt`**, which reaches 94% accuracy on VoxCeleb2/HDTF, ships with an `eval_sync_conf.sh` script, and sits in a repo tagged **openrail++** ([LatentSync](https://github.com/bytedance/LatentSync); [HF](https://huggingface.co/ByteDance/LatentSync-1.6)). OpenRAIL++ generally allows commercial use subject to use-based restrictions, but the actual text has not been read. SyncNet LSE-C/D correlates poorly with human preference ([arXiv 2405.04327](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.04327)), so it works as a floor, not a verdict. The recommended gate layers four checks: a SyncNet confidence threshold and a frame-offset check, both calibrated on accepted and rejected clips (published Sync-C values run about 6.3–8.4); the existing VLM keyframe judge for identity and artifacts; and a licence-clean heuristic that correlates MediaPipe (Apache-2.0) mouth openness with the audio RMS envelope. AV-HuBERT and the Wav2Lip discriminator are believed to be non-commercial, which was not verified, so neither should be adopted.

## Story clips should move to distilled or hosted generation

Wan 2.2 is still the newest Wan with weights, and its **Apache-2.0** licence is the cleanest of the open video models. Wan 3.0 is API-only ([eWeek](https://eweek.com/news/alibaba-wan3-ai-video-documents-apac-china/)). But Wan 2.2 no longer appears on Artificial Analysis's current open-weights image-to-video board. That board is led by **MiniMax H3 (Elo 1137)**, with **LTX-2.5** far behind at 913–914 ([AA open-weights I2V](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/image-to-video/open-weights)). Neither leader suits the engine cleanly. MiniMax H3 is a 33B model whose reference configuration uses four GPUs, and its Community Licence requires users in the **US, EU, UK and South Korea to apply for a licence** ([HF MiniMax-H3](https://huggingface.co/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-H3); [licence Q&A](https://huggingface.co/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-H3/blob/main/docs/QA-about-License.md)). LTX-2.5 runs on 16 GB+ cards with an 8-step distilled transformer, native audio and multi-shot generation. Its licence is free only under **$10M annual recurring revenue**, mandates disclosure, and revokes the licence if watermark or provenance features are removed ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/ltx-2-5-can-generate-a-10-second-ai-video-from-an-image-in-just-6-8-seconds-on-nvidia-superchips-and-its-open-weights); [HF](https://huggingface.co/Lightricks/LTX-2.5)). The engine's disclosure policy already complies. Whether re-encoding for Instagram counts as "removing provenance" is a question for the owner.

Distillation is where the time goes. A community test of Wan 2.2 A14B on one H200 measured **642.8 s for a 720p 5 s clip at baseline and 79.0 s with the 4-step Lightning LoRA** ([Jarvislabs](https://jarvislabs.ai/blog/wan-2-2-lightning-different-video)). lightx2v claims a 100x speedup on Blackwell hardware and publishes 24 GB (RTX 4090) configurations ([HF LightWan2.2](https://huggingface.co/lightx2v/LightWan2.2-A14B); [Wan2.2-Distill-Models](https://huggingface.co/lightx2v/Wan2.2-Distill-Models)). Nobody has verified whether a 4-step LoRA exists for TI2V-5B specifically. If one does, the 14-minute 3090 run should fall to a few minutes. Otherwise A14B with Lightning on a 48 GB card becomes the self-hosted option.

The hosted market undercuts any self-hosting that carries boot overheads. Per a 2026-11-02 secondary roundup of fal prices, a 5 s 720p clip costs about **$0.10 on Wan 2.2 A14B Turbo** (confirmed on fal's T2V page), **$0.20 on MiniMax H3 Max Turbo**, $0.25 on PixVerse C1, $0.35 on Kling 2.6 Pro, $0.56 on Kling 3 Pro, and $1.52–2.37 on Seedance 2.x ([fal Wan Turbo](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/wan/v2.2-a14b/text-to-video/turbo); [TeamDay](https://www.teamday.ai/blog/fal-ai-pricing-2026)). On quality, Seedance 2.5 leads the AA audio I2V board at 1162, but MiniMax H3 at 1137 is within a few dozen Elo for roughly a seventh of the price ([AA I2V](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/image-to-video)). Today's clip costs about $0.05 of 3090 GPU time, which is the project's own figure before boot, download and idle. Self-hosting therefore saves at most tens of dollars a month at hundreds of clips, and it runs a model a generation behind. The scenario below assumes 500 accepted clips a month at an assumed 50% acceptance rate.

| Option | Raw $/clip | Monthly (1,000 generations) | Licence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Self-hosted Wan 2.2 distilled | ~$0.03–0.11 (estimate) | ~$50–100 | Apache-2.0 |
| fal Wan 2.2 A14B Turbo | $0.10 | ~$100 | Apache weights; fal terms |
| MiniMax H3 Max Turbo (hosted) | $0.20 | ~$200 | Provider ToS |
| Kling 3 Pro | $0.56 | ~$560 | Provider ToS |
| Seedance 2.5 | ~$2.37 | ~$2,400 | Provider ToS |

Acceptance rate matters more than sticker price: a $0.40 clip accepted 80% of the time beats a $0.10 clip accepted 15% of the time. Hosted endpoints also churn. fal's Veo 3.1 endpoint ended 2026-11-17, and several promotional prices expired mid-November. That makes the engine's adapter-per-vendor design and a monthly price check mandatory. None of the research covered how hosted video APIs treat uploads of photoreal faces, so refusals could depress acceptance in ways no price table shows.

## Images are cheap already, so upgrade only on measured acceptance

Z-Image Turbo sits at **Elo 936, rank 19 of 36** on AA's open-weights text-to-image board, and costs **$5 per 1k hosted** ([AA open-weights](https://artificialanalysis.ai/image/leaderboard/text-to-image/open-weights)). Self-hosted it renders a 1024² image in about **2.3 s on a 4090** at 8 steps in 16 GB BF16 ([codesota](https://www.codesota.com/news/z-image-turbo-consumer-gpu)), which works out to roughly $0.0002–0.0004 per image at $0.30/h. That is 10–25x cheaper than the cheapest hosted endpoint and over 100x cheaper than frontier APIs such as GPT Image 2.6 (about $211 per 1k) or Nano Banana Pro ($134 per 1k) ([AA overall](https://artificialanalysis.ai/image/leaderboard/text-to-image)).

The one serious open upgrade is **FLUX 3 Image Klein** (Elo 1040, released 2026-10-15). It has 5.6B parameters, needs 11.2 GB in BF16, takes up to 10 reference images, supports LoRA training from day one, and runs in under 9 s on a 4070 in its 16-step distilled INT8 form ([BFL](https://bfl.ai/blog/flux-3-image-klein)). At an interpolated 5–6 s on a 4090 it would still cost under $0.001 per image. Its "FLUX Kommunity License" is free for commercial use only **under $5M annual revenue**, and the blog is silent on output rights ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/black-forest-labs-opens-flux-3-image-klein-weights-with-free-commercial-use-limited-to-smaller-businesses)). The cleanest-licensed model that beats Z-Image Turbo is **HiDream-O1-Image** (Elo 979, 8B, MIT), whose consumer-GPU speed is unmeasured ([HF](https://huggingface.co/HiDream-ai/HiDream-O1-Image)). Several models rank higher but are ruled out on licence. Qwen-Image-2.1 is research-only ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/QwenLM/Qwen-Image-2.1/main/README.md)). FLUX.2 [dev] and klein-9B are non-commercial ([BFL klein](https://bfl.ai/blog/flux2-klein-towards-interactive-visual-intelligence?lid=ECSSqRZRPUuf3RTN)). MiniMax H3-Image needs about 135 GB with its text encoder and has an ambiguous community licence ([HF](https://huggingface.co/MiniMaxAI/minimax-h3-image)). FLUX.2 klein 4B is Apache but scores below Z-Image Turbo (840).

On identity, no rigorous large-sample study compares reference editing with a per-creator LoRA. A one-character pilot scored Seedream 5.0 Pro at 4.86/5 against Nano Banana Pro at 3.89/5 over three scenes ([img.ly](https://img.ly/ai-benchmarks/findings/consistency/)), and practitioner guidance still favours a LoRA for one identity reused across hundreds of scenes ([RunDiffusion](https://learn.rundiffusion.com/consistent-character-template)). That supports keeping the LoRA path. Klein makes a cheap hybrid possible: LoRA plus anchor references in one consumer-GPU model. Two training details matter. Distilled models lose diversity, and the non-distilled Z-Image base is the recommended start point for LoRA training ([GIGAZINE](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260128-z-image-image-generation-ai/)). Training on base and running on Turbo may therefore improve both identity and feed variety, which feed QA would see. For hard repairs, hosted MAI-Image-2.7-Flash (Elo 1159, $10.24 per 1k) and Meta Muse Image (1126, $10 per 1k) give near-frontier quality at about a cent per image ([AA overall](https://artificialanalysis.ai/image/leaderboard/text-to-image)). Their terms for synthetic people are unchecked.

The identity judge needs reinforcement more than the generator does. Research on VLM judges finds holistic pointwise scores suffer "self-consistency collapse," and broad prompts such as "does this preserve identity" produce superficial answers. Pairwise and decomposed feature-level checks work better ([arXiv 2602.06013](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.06013v1); [CHARIS](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08087v1)). The engine correctly avoids InsightFace, whose pretrained models are "for non-commercial research purposes only" ([InsightFace README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/deepinsight/insightface/HEAD/README.md)). **AuraFace** is Apache-2.0, trained on commercially available data, and built on ArcFace principles ([AuraFace card](https://huggingface.co/fal/AuraFace-v1/blob/main/README.md)). Adding its cosine similarity against the creator's anchors as a deterministic score next to the VLM judge would give every benchmark below an objective identity number, with the threshold set in policy. Its accuracy relative to ArcFace was not found, so its threshold should be calibrated against VLM and owner labels. Open models emit no C2PA provenance, while OpenAI and Google outputs carry it ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/advancing-content-provenance); [Vertex](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/content-credentials)). Signing manifests at the "ready" stage would be a cheap way to strengthen the disclosure posture.

## Voices should be designed once, cloned forever, and voiced in one pass

Kokoro (Apache-2.0, Elo 1060, $0.65 per 1M chars hosted) cannot design or clone voices ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard); [Cartesia roundup](https://www.cartesia.ai/learn/open-source-tts)), which is why creators share voices today. Three Apache-2.0 families now cover unique voices end to end. **Qwen3-TTS** ships 1.7B VoiceDesign, CustomVoice and Base models, with natural-language control of "timbre, emotion, and prosody," 3-second cloning and English WER 1.24 ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-VoiceDesign); [tech report](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15621)). **VoxCPM2** (2B) designs a voice from a text description, clones with transcript-aware "ultimate cloning," runs at about **0.30 RTF on a 4090 in about 8 GB VRAM**, and takes up to 8192 tokens per pass ([HF](https://huggingface.co/openbmb/VoxCPM2)). The **MOSS-TTS** family adds VoiceGenerator 1.7B and MOSS-TTSD for "ultra-long dialogues" ([GitHub](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-TTS)). Ming-3.0-tts is reportedly MIT with a "director mode," but that comes from secondary sources only ([kie.ai](https://kie.ai/blog/what-is-ming-3-0)).

Several highly ranked open models are commercially unusable or conditional, and these exclusions matter. Fish S2 Pro, Breeze TTS 2, F5-TTS, Voxtral (open) and XTTS v2 are non-commercial. Chatterbox is MIT but now an unmaintained archive ([Cartesia roundup](https://www.cartesia.ai/learn/open-source-tts)). **Fish Audio Drama 3** is the open-weights leader at Elo 1277 and the most expressive, with inline tags and free-text direction. Its Community Licence is free commercially only **under US$500k revenue**, requires "Built with Fish Audio" attribution, and its licence name conflicts between README and HF tag ([AA open-weights](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard/open-weights); [magica](https://magica.com/news/fish-audio-drama-3-open-weights-commercial-limits)).

TTS price barely matters at this volume. ElevenLabs v4 leads the arena at 1340 Elo for $80 per 1M chars ([ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing/api)). At an assumed 900 chars per spoken minute, 1,000 one-minute talking videos would cost about $72 a month even there, a small sum next to lip-sync GPU time. The real arguments for self-hosting are uniqueness, control and avoiding terms risk on hosted synthetic-persona clauses, none of which were read. The workflow follows from that. Generate N candidate voices from the creator bible's description, let the judge pick one, and store a 10–20 s voice anchor plus transcript in the reference pack next to the image anchors. Then voice every script by zero-shot cloning of that synthetic clip, which also avoids real-person consent issues.

That workflow also fixes the per-segment intonation reset. TTS should run **once per full 60–90 s script**, and the audio, not the text, should be split at pauses into lip-sync segments. Qwen3-TTS's 12 Hz tokenizer puts 90 s at roughly 1,080 audio tokens, comfortably one pass (an inference from the tokenizer rate). A speaker-embedding distance check between creators' anchors would catch designed voices that cluster. No source covers this; it is a design suggestion.

## The Brain should cache and batch before it switches models

Anthropic's current prices are **Opus 5.5 at $4/$20, Sonnet 5.5 at $2/$10 and Haiku 5.5 at $0.10/$0.50** per 1M tokens (for prompts up to 100K tokens). Batch takes 50% off, and cache hits on Opus and Sonnet 5.5 cost **0.05x input**. The two discounts stack ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). Caching the stable persona bible, rubrics and system prompts, and sending non-urgent evaluator and idea-scorer work through Batch, should roughly halve the heaviest roles' bill with no quality risk. That makes it the first move, ahead of any benchmark. One counter-intuitive data point is that Sonnet 5.5 at max effort costs about the same per Artificial Analysis index task as Opus 5.5 ($10.02 vs $10.04) ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models)). For low-volume strategist and creator-lab calls, Opus at a fixed effort may cost no more than Sonnet.

Open models win on price at the top tier, not the bottom. Haiku 5.5 (index 36) matches or undercuts the cheap open models: DeepSeek V4.1 Flash costs $0.10/$0.29 on DeepInfra at index 33 ([DeepInfra](https://deepinfra.com/pricing)). The open frontier still trails: Kimi K3.1 Pro scores 46 against Sonnet 5.5's 55. On creative writing, Claude leads human votes, with Fable 5 first on the arena.ai board, while GLM-5.3-max ranks 13th and Kimi K3-max 23rd. Kimi K3 and GLM-5.3 sit about 45 Elo behind Claude Opus 5 on EQ-Bench, which itself uses a Claude judge ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/which-ai-model-writes-best-leaderboards-disagree)). That supports keeping Claude as the persona voice and shadow-testing **GLM-5.3** for the showrunner. GLM-5.3 costs $0.56/$2.50 on DeepInfra, about 4x cheaper on output than Sonnet. For vision judging, **Qwen3-VL-235B** costs $0.20/$0.88 on DeepInfra, roughly 10x cheaper than Sonnet. Research shows VLMs struggle with anatomy and aesthetics ([AAAI 2026](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/39666)), so hands and anatomy rubric items should stay on Claude. DeepInfra advertises strict `json_schema` enforcement, while Together lists structured outputs without a strict guarantee ([DeepInfra docs](https://docs.deepinfra.com/chat/structured-outputs); [Together docs](https://docs.together.ai/docs/json-mode)). Validation and the Claude fallback should stay either way.

The most cost-efficient proprietary alternative found is **GPT-6.1 Terra** ($1/$5, index 51, $0.96 per index task) ([OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)). Using it needs a new host entry, and its creative-writing standing is unknown. Gemini 3.6/3.8 Flash and Gemini 4 Carbon/Argon should be avoided because their prices double on 2027-01-01 and 2026-12-01 respectively ([Google AI Studio](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)). The weight-licence gates on Kimi (display clause at scale) and GLM-5.3 (security review above $10B revenue) only apply at scales far beyond Manyworlds, and DeepSeek V4 is plain MIT ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/zai-glm-weights-license/); [Forkast](https://forkast.news/chinese-open-weight-frontier-compresses-five-labs-thirty-days-two-licensing-models/)).

## Prioritized benchmark plan

Priority follows from where the gate currently accepts nothing or costs the most per acceptance. Talking video runs only on the owner's go, as CLAUDE.md requires, and every arm records cost per accepted output in the ledger under its policy version. Sample sizes are suggestions sized for a first read, not powered experiments.

| # | What | Arms | Hardware | Judged by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Brain cost cut (config, no benchmark) | Prompt caching on bibles, rubrics and system prompts; Batch for evaluator and idea scorer | Control plane | Ledger $/decision before vs after; `mw brain rubric-drift` shows no drift |
| 1 | Lip-sync scorer in the video gate (prerequisite for 2 and 5) | LatentSync `stable_syncnet.pt` confidence and offset, MediaPipe mouth/RMS heuristic | CPU or co-located on talk worker | Threshold calibrated on hosted clips as a reference (WaveSpeed InfiniteTalk-Fast, Hedra) plus owner labels |
| 2 | Talking video unblock (owner's go) | InfiniteTalk + lightx2v 4-step, fp8 single checkpoint, 480p then 720p; ~20 clips × 6–30 s | RunPod/Vast 4090 or L40S, >48 GB host RAM | Video gate (identity, artifacts) + scorer from #1; wall-clock per output second; boot download size |
| 3 | Story clip speed | Wan 2.2 TI2V-5B or A14B + Lightning/lightx2v 4-step (self-hosted) vs fal Wan 2.2 Turbo, MiniMax H3 Max Turbo, PixVerse C1, Kling 2.6 Pro; ~30 clips per arm from the same keyframes | 3090/4090 for 5B; 48 GB card for A14B; hosted adapters | Video gate identity vs source keyframe + AuraFace on sampled frames; refusal rate; $/accepted clip incl. boot and idle |
| 4 | Unique voices + single-pass scripts | Qwen3-TTS VoiceDesign→Base clone, VoxCPM2 design→ultimate clone vs Kokoro; full 60–90 s scripts in one pass | Co-located on talk worker or 3090 | Evaluator rubric on persona fit and prosody; speaker-embedding distance between creators; downstream lip-sync score |
| 5 | Talking video alternates | SoulX-FlashTalk (Apache), LongCat-Video-Avatar 1.6 (MIT), two-stage silent clip → InfiniteTalk V2V or MuseTalk | Same as #2 | Same as #2, plus body/gesture match on monologue formats |
| 6 | Image identity upgrade | AuraFace similarity added to image gate first; then Z-Image Turbo + LoRA (base-trained) vs FLUX 3 Klein + LoRA + anchor refs (licence permitting) vs HiDream-O1; ~100 prompts per creator | 3090/4090 | Image gate acceptance, AuraFace cosine vs anchors, feed-QA diversity; $/accepted image |
| 7 | Brain shadow tests | Haiku 5.5 for idea scorer and analyst summaries; GLM-5.3 and Kimi K3.1 Pro for the showrunner; Qwen3-VL-235B for the evaluator with pairwise or decomposed prompts | Together/DeepInfra via router | `mw llm shadow-report`, `mw brain calibrate` agreement with Claude and owner labels; JSON validity rate |

Talking video (#2) goes ahead of story clips (#3) because it is the only format producing nothing, and its fix needs no new licence. Images come late because the current stage is already cheap and working. An upgrade there pays only if acceptance rises enough to beat a cost that is already near zero.

## Conclusion

Two things changed in the second half of 2026. Step distillation turned 40-step video and avatar models into 4–8-step ones, and hosted endpoints repriced to within a few cents of well-run self-hosting. Those shifts move the engine's question from "which model is best" to "which stage is wasting GPU time and where does the gate reject." The cheapest wins are settings and plumbing: a LoRA flag on InfiniteTalk, one TTS pass per script, prompt caching. They need neither a new model nor a new licence. Benchmarks do the rest, and only if the gates measure what Elo does not. That means adding a deterministic identity score (AuraFace) and a lip-sync score before the model bake-offs, so that each bake-off ends in a number the ledger can rank.

The licence landscape is now the true selection filter. The commercially clean open stack is Apache/MIT throughout: Wan 2.2, Z-Image, InfiniteTalk, SoulX-FlashTalk, LongCat 1.6, Qwen3-TTS, VoxCPM2, DeepSeek V4. Everything above it in quality either sits behind a revenue cap (FLUX 3 Klein at $5M, LTX-2.5 at $10M, Drama 3 at $500k), a territory gate (MiniMax H3), or a hosted ToS nobody has read yet. The caps cost nothing while the engine is pre-revenue, but each one is a future migration. The owner should accept them deliberately and write each threshold into policy, so the engine flags its own licence cliffs before it reaches them.
