# The autonomous talent agency: assessment and build plan (2026-11-23)

The owner's brief, "Manyworlds: The Autonomous AI Talent Agency", is read here against what runs today. This doc
gives my view, then what gets built tonight and what waits.

## My view in one paragraph

The org chart is right as a map of **responsibilities**, but most of its fifteen functions should not be agents.
An agent earns its place when a decision needs judgement over messy evidence and has a clear owner. Everything
else is better as code: deterministic, cheap, testable, never "creative" about money or safety. That means
**four judgement roles that own decisions**: the CEO, Market Leads, Talent Managers, and the Creative team. They
sit on **a bench of specialists**, half of which are already code. What is genuinely missing is not more agents
but the **connective tissue**: typed requests between roles with explicit decision rights, recorded predictions that
are scored against real outcomes, and creator-level resources a manager can ask for. Without those, the agents
write reports to each other. With them, the agents form an organisation that can disagree, decide, and learn
whether its decisions were any good.

## 2. Answers to the ten questions

1. **Already covered:** CEO, scouting, creative, evaluation, experiments, production, QA, learning, memory,
   publishing. Partly: talent manager (tonight), finance (numbers exist, no check), market intelligence (data
   without an owner).
2. **Genuinely missing:**
   (a) a way for roles to ask each other for things, with an owner who decides;
   (b) decision rights per role, enforced in code;
   (c) creator-level resources (tiers) a manager can argue for;
   (d) predictions that are scored, the only proof that judgement improves;
   (e) an owner per market;
   (f) a monitor that keeps the organisation from stalling.
3. **Yes: the talent manager is each creator's coordinator.** The showrunner plans from its brief, events about
   the creator route to it, and requests about the creator come from it. The CEO never talks to the showrunner.
4. **Market Leads are activated, not appointed.** The CEO covers every market until a market holds
   `brain.market_lead.min_creators` creators or the CEO opens it explicitly. Then a weekly Market Lead run owns
   the market thesis (`markets.dossier`, conviction), proposes cohorts to the CEO, and passes learnings between
   its creators. The interaction is one way down and one way up: Leads send typed requests to the CEO, and the CEO
   decides them in its daily run.
5. **Specialists are invoked by request, return structured results, and share memory through the database:**
   findings, briefs, the dossier, decisions. A request has a question, a requester, an owner and a due date. Its
   answer lands where the requester's next run reads it. No agent waits on another synchronously.
6. **Debate is requests plus counter-evidence, resolved once.**
   - A talent manager disagreeing with the CEO files a request with its conviction and an experiment plan.
   - The CEO accepts or rejects it with a reason.
   - A rejected request may be filed again only with new evidence, or after `cooldown_days`. That rules out loops.
   - QA is the exception: it is a gate, not a party to debate.
7. **Decision rights live in policy (`brain.rights`) and are checked in code:**
   - **Talent managers** decide their creator's creative direction, experiments (within the experiment caps) and
     research requests (within a weekly allowance).
   - **The CEO** decides tiers, launches (within pacing), stage moves and market openings.
   - **The owner** decides anything above the master budget, retirements, and launches while approval is required.
   - **Market leads** recommend only.
   - **Finance** can veto, but only on numbers.
8. **Scale comes from schedule by stage plus events, cheap by default.**
   - No process runs per creator. A talent manager is a row of state (profile, dossier, brief, conviction) plus a
     job when it is due.
   - Exploring creators get a review weekly, growing creators twice a week, paused creators monthly.
   - A drop or a breakout triggers a run at once.
   - Routine reviews use a mid-tier model; diagnosis can use a stronger one.
   - Per-creator-day budgets bound the total. At 1,000 creators and about $0.05 per review, weekly reviews cost
     about $7 a day.
9. **What would most improve discovery and growth:**
   (a) the predictions ledger;
   (b) market-scope and portfolio-scope findings written by the analyst, so one creator's discovery reaches others;
   (c) a negative-results memory (failed hypotheses stay findable);
   (d) creator tiers, so winners get better production;
   (e) later, a lookalike model that scores a concept against real creators' trajectories (needs data first).
10. **Showing that decisions get better over time:** every role writes predictions with a horizon:
    - a talent manager's conviction and hypotheses
    - the evaluator's scores
    - the CEO's continue/stop calls
    - a market lead's thesis

    A resolver scores them against real metrics only. The scorecard (`mw brain scorecard`) shows calibration and
    hit rate per role and per month. "The organisation got smarter" then means "its predictions got better
    calibrated", not "it ran more jobs". Simulated outcomes resolve only sandbox predictions, labelled as such.

## 3. What gets built tonight (in order)

1. **Requests** (`proposals` table, `brain/proposals.py`):
   - kinds: `tier_change`, `budget`, `research`, `experiment`, `cohort`, `production_upgrade`, `escalate`
   - each has a requester, an owner (CEO, a market, or the owner), evidence, conviction, status, resolution and
     reason
   - checked by `brain.rights` and finance; deduplicated, with a cooldown after a rejection
2. **Predictions** (`predictions` table, `brain/predictions.py`):
   - recorded by the talent manager and the CEO
   - resolved by a deterministic job from real metrics (or labelled sandbox)
   - `mw brain scorecard`
3. **Talent manager v2:**
   - it states its conviction (with an interval and a rationale), so a weak creator with real upside can be
     argued for
   - its requests become typed proposals
   - its hypotheses become predictions
   - a `diagnose` mode runs when a drop event arrives (events route `drop` to it) and can commission the analyst
4. **CEO v2:** the strategist reads open requests addressed to it and decides each one; code applies what rights
   and finance allow (tiers on `creator_states.tier`); it sees market theses.
5. **Creator tiers** (`brain.tiers`): economy, standard and premium set the posting cap, the video share cap and
   the production routes. The CEO sets the tier; the talent manager can only ask for a change.
6. **Market lead** (`agents/market_lead.py`):
   - activated by policy
   - writes the market thesis and conviction, and proposes cohorts and requests to the CEO
   - reads trend briefs, market findings and its creators' briefs
7. **Chief of staff** (`brain/chief_of_staff.py`, code only):
   - requests past due, agent jobs failing repeatedly, agents spending with nothing to show, owner events
   - runs in the producer tick

Not tonight:
- the brand strategist and commercial roles
- a separate growth agent (merged into the analyst)
- lookalike scoring
- giving the analyst a market-scope job of its own (the market lead reads creator findings until then)

## 4. Rules that still bind
- Simulated evidence never counts as demand.
- QA cannot be overridden.
- Money above the agents' rights goes to the owner.
- No cross-account engagement.
- Launches and `operations.mode` are the owner's.
- Every decision is recorded with its cost.
